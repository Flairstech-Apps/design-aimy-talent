Seven ways to draw where a lead stands

Today the block draws the moments that already happened as a chain of dots: Already a contact, First called, Callback. It never shows the ladder those moments sit on, so it can't say how far the lead has come or how far it has to go. Each iteration below is a working mock drawn from Sales's own ladder (LADDER in bdr.js): Not called, No answer, Callback, Answered, Meeting set, Showed up, Interested, Handed over, plus the three exits. Use the switch to run every mock against three leads. Iteration 1 is the recommendation.

The moments, without the ladder

What works

  • The sentence says the state and when.
  • The open tip in the step's tone marks where it is now.
  • Every dot is a fact that happened, with a date and who.

What is missing

  • No sense of distance: is Callback early or late? The ladder isn't drawn.
  • Nothing about time: 8 days waiting on a callback looks the same as 1.
  • What happens next sits in a separate row below, apart from the picture.
1 · Recommended

The track: the whole ladder, with the moments on it

All eight steps on one line, like an order tracker. Steps passed are filled, the current one is an open ring in its tone, steps ahead stay hollow. A dashed mark shows where the caller's part ends and the director takes over. The dates sit under the steps they belong to; moments that aren't steps (Already a contact, First called) become small chips under the track. An exit branches off the line.

Inspired by

Walmart · order trackerfilled past, current dot, hollow ahead Amazon · order cardfour steps, the current one named above Zillow · lease stepslabels under every step, the last ringed Carbon · progress indicatorcompleted, current, not started, error

Works

  • Answers "where, how far, how far to go" in one row.
  • Keeps Sales's own vocabulary: dots, the open tip, the tone.
  • The hand-off line says whose job each part is.

Falls short

  • Eight labels need about 640px; it scrolls on a phone.
  • Repeated no-answers fold into one step.
2

The path: chevrons, with coaching for the step

Salesforce's Path. The ladder as chevrons, the current one filled in its tone with the days spent in it. Under it, the step's coaching: what to do now, and what moves the lead on. The moments become the chevrons' dates.

Inspired by

Salesforce · lead pathchevrons, a check on what's done Pipedrive · deal stagesdays in each stage on the chevron Zoho CRM · migration stepschevrons with check and current SLDS · Pathstages plus a coaching panel for the step

Works

  • The most recognised CRM pattern; nothing to learn.
  • Coaching turns the state into what to do.

Falls short

  • Heavy: a coloured bar dominates the page.
  • Loses who did what; only dates fit.
  • Looks clickable, though a step is moved by a call, not a press.
3

The days: each step as long as it took

A bar whose segments are as wide as the days spent in each step, the current one striped and still growing. A dashed mark inside it shows the step's usual time, so a stalled lead shows itself. The steps still ahead are listed as chips.

Inspired by

Pipedrive · days per stagetime is the stage's label HubSpot · time in current stage"stalled" at 20% past the usual Pipedrive · contacts timelineactivity laid along real time

Works

  • Makes a stuck lead impossible to miss.
  • Same picture as the Reports page's ruler in Talent.

Falls short

  • Short steps get too narrow to label.
  • Needs a "usual" per step, which Sales doesn't keep yet (the mock uses sample norms).
4

The timeline: what happened, now, and what comes next

Vertical, like a delivery or application tracker. Done steps are compact; the current step is large, with the owed task inside it; the steps ahead are dotted, each with its usual time. Reads top to bottom on any width.

Inspired by

Hers · treatment timelinebig current step, "avg 12 hrs" on what's next Mercury · application timelinedone, awaiting, ahead in one column Stripe · task progressthree states, vertical, beside the detail Wise · transfer updatesdated steps, then the actions

Works

  • Joins the picture and the next task, now two rows apart.
  • Works at any width, phone included.

Falls short

  • Tall: eight steps take a column of the page.
  • The ladder's shape is less obvious than on a line.
5

The compact line: the state, eight ticks, and the moments behind a press

For tight places: the contact's peek, a list row, a card. The state in its tone, eight small ticks for the ladder, "3 of 8", and the moments folded behind a press. Shown as the block and as cards.

Inspired by

Apple · order at a glancethe state as a sentence, a thin bar under it Coinbase · application"50% complete" over the steps Mercor · processing stepsthree steps, one line each

Works

  • Fits a row, a peek and a card with the same parts.
  • One line of ticks reads at a glance across a list.

Falls short

  • Too quiet for the contact page itself.
  • Ticks have no labels; hover or the press says which.
6

The change log: every move, who made it and from what

The story as a record of changes, newest first: "Not called → Callback, by Engy, 27 Oct". Each line shows the step before and after as chips, so nothing about how the lead moved is left out, including moves back.

Inspired by

Attio · record activity"Stage → Meeting", attribute by attribute Pipedrive · history"Stage: Qualified → Demo Scheduled" HubSpot · logged call with outcomethe call and what it changed

Works

  • Honest about moves back and repeat no-answers.
  • Who did each move is always there.

Falls short

  • A log, not a picture: no sense of distance to the end.
  • Duplicates the touchpoints list lower on the page.
7

Among everyone: the campaign's funnel, with this lead on it

The ladder as the campaign's funnel, one bar per step with how many leads are at it now, and this lead marked on its step: "one of 37 waiting on a callback". It places one lead against the rest, which a single lead's line cannot.

Inspired by

QuickBooks · customers funnelcounts at each stage, left to right Zendesk Sell · pipeline flowwhere deals moved between stages Juicebox · board by statuseach person as a card in a stage

Works

  • Context a single line can't give: is this lead normal?
  • A door to the others at the same step.

Falls short

  • About the campaign, not the person: it belongs beside the story, not instead of it.
  • Counts in the mock are samples.

The recommendation

Iteration 1 on the contact page: it keeps today's vocabulary and adds what is missing, the ladder and the hand-off. Borrow two things: from 3, flag a step stuck past its usual time on the current step's label ("8 days, usually 2"); from 4, draw the vertical version below 640px instead of scrolling. Iteration 5 is the same component for list rows and peeks, so the ladder looks the same everywhere it appears.
