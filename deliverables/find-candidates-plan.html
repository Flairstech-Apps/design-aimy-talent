A new plan for the search

The Find page asks three questions in three rows of identical chips, then puts the part that decides the results, what the person must have, last. This plan turns it into one sentence you can read back, the needs right under it, and a count of who already fits before you press anything.

What gets in the way today

Read off the live page and its code (findPage(), critBlock(), FIND_SOURCES).

Bug

The Find button sits under the AiMY bar

The only action on the page is at the bottom left, and the floating “Ask AiMY” composer covers it at common window heights.

Hierarchy

Every choice looks the same

“Start from”, the requisitions under it, and “Where to look” are all the same chip. The answer row reads as a fourth question, and the chosen chip is only a faint purple.

Scale

A wall of requisitions

Omar has 5 open requisitions; Dina sees 13, three of them “Bilingual Technical Support (German)”. A chip per requisition wraps into rows and can’t tell the three apart.

Focus

The needs come last

“Read against” decides who comes back, yet it is the fourth block. Required and Preferred sit 900px to the right of the line they belong to.

Feedback

No idea what you’ll get

Nothing says how many people in the pool already fit until you search. Making a need required or preferred changes nothing you can see.

Concepts

Sources mix two ideas

Apollo doesn’t find anyone; it adds a work email to people the web search found. SignalHire is shown only to say it’s off.

The plan

Four rules every direction below follows.

Say the search as a sentenceWho it’s like, which role, where to look, written as one line you can read back and change in place.
The needs are the centreFour to six lines, each with Required or Preferred right beside it, and how many in the pool show it.
Count before you searchThe pool is ours, so the count of who fits updates as you edit. The web costs time, so it runs on the button.
One action, always visibleA bar pinned to the bottom of the search, above AiMY’s composer, with the count and Find candidates.
Start from (who left, a requisition, or text)→ The needs, with live pool count→ Where to look→ Find candidates→ Pool first, then web prospects→ Add to the requisition, or save the search

Three directions

Each is drawn with real data from the prototype. The controls work: change a need, a source or a tab and the mockup answers.

The three chip rows become one sentence with three pickers. Each picker is a short searchable menu, so 13 requisitions fit and the three German ones show their account and date. The needs follow as a list, and a bar at the bottom says how many already fit.

C · AiMY drafts it, you adjust

How you arrive

Most searches start from somewhere: a requisition’s Find candidates button, the Today tile, a chat. Instead of an empty form, AiMY opens with the search already drafted and says where each part came from. One press runs it; Change it opens direction A, filled in.

Recommendation

Build A as the page, open it the way C does, and add B’s side-by-side results on wide screens. A fixes the bug and the hierarchy on every screen size. C removes the setup for the common case without adding a second form. B is a layout of A, not a separate design, so it can come last.

Build order

Each step ships on its own and leaves the page better than before.

Step 1

Pin the action bar

Move Find candidates, Save this search and the count into a bar at the bottom of the search block, sitting above AiMY’s composer. Fixes the hidden button. In findPage(), the t-acts row becomes the bar.

Step 2

The sentence with pickers

Replace the Start from chips and the requisition and leaver chip rows with the brief: three tokens, each opening a searchable menu. “Describe or paste” turns the sentence into “Find people who…” with the text box under it.

Step 3

Needs with their counts

In critBlock(), put Required or Preferred beside each line and show how many in the pool show it, amber when it’s fewer than five. Use the pool data only.

Step 4

Sources as switches

Pool and public web as two switches; Apollo as “Add work emails” that only enables with the web. SignalHire leaves the row and moves to the footnote. FIND_SOURCES keeps its keys.

Step 5

Arrive drafted

The requisition’s Find candidates, the Today tile and the chat open the AiMY card first, with Run it and Change it.

Step 6

Results beside the search

At 1100px and up, the results card sits right of the search and the pool tab updates live. Below that, it stays under the search as now.

The counts in the mockups are illustrative, worked from the prototype’s sample pool. No fit percentages anywhere: each person shows how many of the needs they meet, with Why behind it.
