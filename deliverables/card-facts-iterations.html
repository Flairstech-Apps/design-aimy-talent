The card's facts, four ways to calm them

A recruiter said they were lost in the card's white and grey writing. The facts block is where it bunches up: on a card four to a row it runs to three or four lines with four icons, the languages wrap onto a line of their own, and the icons mark plain data (years, place) exactly as they mark the one thing that changes what you do (Returning). Each iteration below changes only that block, on three real cards at the 4-column width.

Cards are drawn with Talent's own CSS.

What the iterations are held to

  • Icons mark meaning, not data (ui-ux-pro-max: an icon earns its place when it carries meaning; impeccable craft floor: no decoration standing in for content). Years and place read without a drawing; Returning and the interview news are the signals.
  • Group by proximity, separate by space (ui-ux-pro-max, layout): one line of data, then a line of signal, not a ragged wrap that splits a fact from its neighbours.
  • No eyebrow above a heading (impeccable craft floor). "From the talent pool" sits above the name; iteration 4 tries it without.
  • Contrast and size floor: every run stays at 13px or more and 4.5:1 or better in both themes; the dots are separators, a step quieter than the words (web interface guidelines; AiMY's ink band).
  • Real copy at the real width: the long place name ("6th of October") and the two-language list are the cases that wrap, so they are in every row.
