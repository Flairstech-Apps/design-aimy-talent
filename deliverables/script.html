AiMY Talent · what to say

Presentation script

20 slides · about 22 minutes, plus an optional 5-minute live demo and questions · deck: “AiMY Talent” on claude.ai · read the prep pack for the facts behind every line.

How to use this. The words in normal type are what you say. Purple lines are cues: when to click, when to pause, what to point at. Times are a guide; the research section (slides 1–6) should take about 7 minutes, the product (7–16) about 11, and the close (17–20) about 4. If you are short of time, skip slide 6 (the loop is also on slide 5) and shorten slides 11 and 13.

1 · Closing the hiring loop

0:00 · 45 s

Good morning, and thank you for the time. Today I’ll cover three things. First, what our research found about how recruiting works today, here and in the market. Second, what we built in AiMY Talent because of it. And third, the few decisions we need from you to move forward.

Everything you’ll see on screen is the working prototype. It runs on sample data, with invented people, so nothing here is a real candidate.

Click.

2 · Today’s funnel: 268 screened, 21 hired

0:45 · 1 min 15 s

Let’s start with our own numbers. This is our recruitment dashboard for August and September: 39 requests, 45 vacancies, and six recruiters.

Point along the cards from left to right.

268 people passed a phone screen. 190 went through HR, 102 through the technical interview, 81 were sent to a client, 24 met the client, and 21 were hired. Each percentage is the share of the stage before.

Point at the three times.

Now look at time. It takes 14 days before a client sees a first profile; that is the number clients actually feel. 32 days to hire. And 54 days until the person starts work. Joining, not accepting, is the real finish line.

Click.

3 · The client isn’t the main leak

2:00 · 1 min

Our first instinct was that clients reject too much: only 30% of the profiles we send get an interview. But staffing firms usually send three to five profiles for every client interview. We send 3.4, which is normal. And clients then hired 21 of the 24 people they met.

So the real losses are somewhere else. Upstream: of 268 people we screen, only 81 ever reach a client. And in time: 14 days before the client sees anyone.

One honest caveat: that 3-to-5 benchmark comes from vendor blogs and an old survey. Treat it as a direction, not proof. That is why the first test I’ll propose is to code the real client feedback.

Click.

4 · Single steps are solved. The loop isn’t.

3:00 · 1 min 15 s

We researched eight areas and about 45 products: applicant tracking systems, staffing tools, AI sourcing, assessments and AI interviewers, interview notetakers, analytics, the market, and the law.

Every single step is crowded and well funded. Search is solved. AI interviews are solved. Notetakers are solved. What nobody does is join the steps into one loop that learns.

Point at each card.

Four gaps stand out. One: when a client says no, nobody records why in a way matching can learn from. Two: nobody keeps a skills record that interviews confirm over time; it’s always what the CV says. Three: nobody checks which test questions actually predict who clients accept. And four: almost nobody measures who is still employed at day 90 and feeds that back. Only one in five US companies even measured it last year.

Click.

5 · Our opening

4:15 · 1 min 15 s

Pause and let the sentence land before you speak.

So here is our opening. An agent-run recruiting team for outsourcing firms, paid per qualified candidate, and measured on who is still there at day 90.

We can do this when others can’t, for three reasons. We own the whole funnel, from sourcing to the client’s decision to day 90; a tool that owns one step can’t close the loop. We hire in Arabic and European languages, and no vendor scores spoken German or Arabic. And AiMY does the coordination, the scheduling, the chasing, the updates, while people make every decision about people.

One note on price: we should compare ourselves to recruiting services, about three to ten thousand dollars per hire, not to software seats.

Click.

6 · The loop we close

5:30 · 1 min 15 s

This is the loop, in six steps: the request, finding people, judging them, the client, the offer and joining, and day 90.

Point at the three loops at the bottom.

And three things feed back. First, every client decision carries a reason, so matching learns what “fit” means for that client. Second, every interview confirms or contradicts what the CV says, so the next requisition starts from what we already know; 82% of our requests replace someone, so that matters. Third, day-90 check-ins feed changes to the success profile, which an HR Manager approves, and only once there are at least 20 of them, because a pattern needs data.

That’s the research. Now let me show you what we built.

Click.

7 · One product on AiMY Sales, five desks

6:45 · 1 min

AiMY Talent is built on the AiMY Sales shell and components, so it looks and behaves like the rest of the AiMY family.

It has five desks. The recruiter, Omar, does the daily work across eight tabs. The hiring manager, Rania, gives debriefs and approves offers. Dina, the HR Manager, sees every requisition and approves what AiMY proposes to learn. Hesham, the BU Director, answers for budget and headcount. And Jana, at the client, sees only the profiles we send her.

Point at the rules line at the bottom.

Everywhere, the same rules: one press with Undo, never an “are you sure” box; evidence as counts; unknown said as unknown; and a person makes every decision.

Click.

8 · Today: a briefing, not a list

7:45 · 1 min 15 s

This is where a recruiter starts the day. Not a list of records, a briefing. Four decisions wait on you, the oldest three days. Two interviews today. One requisition likely to miss its date, and why.

Each decision is one press, and each can be undone. Below, every block shows three items and what waiting costs, in days past the usual time. Our research found 46 of 105 lost days were spent waiting for feedback, so we make that visible.

Before an interview, Prepare me writes the brief: what to probe, what’s already confirmed. And when someone isn’t progressing, the answer they’ll get is shown before it goes, under the HR template.

Click.

9 · Candidates: evidence you can check

9:00 · 1 min 15 s

Every candidate is scored as a count: three of four must-haves. Never a percentage. Opaque scores are a legal target, and research shows people follow an AI pick about nine times in ten, even when it’s wrong. So the evidence is built to be checked.

Point at a “Not known” row.

A must-have the CV doesn’t mention reads as “not known,” not as missing, and it comes with a way to find out: ask the candidate, or check it in the next interview.

Point at Confirmed and Differs.

And here is the record nobody else keeps: for each must-have, what the CV says and what the interview found, with one status, confirmed or differs.

Click.

10 · Requisitions: a forecast on every card

10:15 · 1 min 15 s

Each requisition says when it’s likely to fill at this pace, against the date we promised, and how long the first profile took against a seven-day target.

Because 82% of our requests replace someone, a new requisition starts there: pick the person who left, and the role, the must-haves and the account come with them. Or paste the notes from the kickoff call, and AiMY drafts it and tells you what the notes didn’t say.

And approval only happens by exception: a new seat, a budget above its band, three or more seats, or an unbilled client role. Everything else opens straight away, and the right person is asked only when it matters.

Click.

11 · Calls: a call, logged in a sentence

11:30 · 1 min

The phone screen is where most people are lost, so Calls is built for speed. Each row tells you what to get from that call: the notice, the expected salary, and whatever the CV is silent on.

When you press Call, AiMY opens a brief. When you hang up, it reads back what it heard, “I read that as passed the screen, is that right?”, and logging is one press, or one sentence to correct it.

The recruiter places every call; nothing dials by itself. Callbacks have the window the person asked for. After no answer, one short message names the role. And in a run of calls, AiMY coaches one habit at a time.

Click.

12 · Diary: the week, and the results not in

12:30 · 45 s

Today is what needs you now. The Diary is the week and the month: interviews with Prepare me, callbacks, start dates, and the day-30 and day-90 check-ins.

Point at Results not in.

And underneath, the list that matters most: every interview that happened with no result yet, oldest first, each with one nudge. Waiting for feedback is where we lose the most days, so this is where to look first.

13 · Find: pool first, criteria you can change

13:15 · 1 min

Finding people starts from what’s most common for us: someone like the person who left. Their role’s must-haves become the criteria, and you see them before you search. Change any line, and mark it required or preferred.

We search our own pool first, people who applied before, then public web pages. Never LinkedIn scraping. Every web fact shows its page and the date, and anyone can report wrong data. A person found on the web is a prospect until they reply, and first contact needs a person’s approval.

Saved searches run again each time, and tell you when new people in the pool match.

Click.

14 · Assessments: short, fair and checked

14:15 · 1 min

Each test carries a checklist from the research: about ten to fifteen minutes, works on a phone, a practice question first, and a short note to everyone after.

Integrity, for example an answer pasted in, is said on its own line and never changes the score. A person looks.

The German check gives a level from A1 to C2, because our requisitions ask for German at C1, and no vendor scores spoken German. And before anything is scored, the candidate is told AiMY scores and a person decides, and can choose a person instead.

Click.

15 · The client desk: a reason on every “no”

15:15 · 1 min

This is the first loop in action. The client has a desk and sees only the profiles we send, each with a write-up the recruiter approved.

They meet someone, or pass with one reason from a short list: spoken language, experience, salary, notice, or skills. None of the eight staffing tools we studied records this.

Once reasons add up, AiMY can say, for example, “most of this client’s declines mention spoken German,” and propose checking spoken German on the phone screen before a profile goes to them. The HR Manager approves it; nothing changes on its own. And the client never sees salary, margin or our notes.

Click.

16 · Reports: one definition per number

16:15 · 1 min

Today’s dashboard says 21 hired in one place and 13 in another: two different units under one label. So every number here carries one definition, its unit, where it starts and stops, behind “How it is counted.”

Each measure has a target and a month-by-month trend. We added the staffing measures the dashboard lacks, profiles per hire, declined offers, and billed hires next to who stayed to day 90, and “where time goes” shows days lost by step.

And it shows team views only. Research found ranking people cut productivity by 14%, so coaching one person at a time lives in AiMY QA.

Click. You are now about 17 minutes in.

17 · Trust and the law

17:15 · 1 min

One thing is urgent, and it isn’t a design decision. Egypt’s data protection law has a one-year grace period that ends around the first of November. FlairsTech needs a licence, a registered data protection officer, and a permit to move client data across borders. Please take it to legal this week.

Everything else is built in. Scores are evidence a person checks, and nobody is rejected without a person. We ask consent before contact, recording and AI scoring. WhatsApp only after opt-in. No LinkedIn scraping. We never read emotion from a face or a voice. And a candidate can have their details deleted.

19 · Five tests before we build further

19:30 · 1 min

Before we build further, five small tests, each one deciding something.

And ask two account managers how clients send feedback today.

If you get asked…

short answers
  • “Is this live?” No, it’s a working prototype on sample data; the integrations are placeholders. It lets us test with recruiters before engineering builds it.
  • “Why no match percentage?” Legal risk, and people over-trust AI picks. A count with evidence invites a check.
  • “What does AiMY decide alone?” Nothing about a person. It drafts, reads back, nudges and proposes; a person presses every move.
  • “How is this different from Bullhorn?” They track steps; we close the loop: client reasons, verified skills, outcome-linked questions, day 90, in Arabic and German.
  • “Why not rank recruiters?” Rank incentives cut productivity 14% in a field experiment; individual coaching lives in AiMY QA.
  • “LinkedIn? WhatsApp?” No scraping; WhatsApp only after opt-in.
  • “Price?” Per qualified candidate, compared with recruiting services, not software.
