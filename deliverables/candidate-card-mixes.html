Decision first, with the story in it

Seven ways to join the two cards you picked from the last sheet: 1 · Decision first (the stage and the verb frame the card, equal heights, AiMY's sentence kept) and 6 · The story card (the requisition's stages as a small track, the current one named with what comes next, and the last touch). Each is drawn with the same six candidates from Omar Saeed's queue. Mix A is the closest join; mix D is the recommendation.

4 Nov 2026 From deliverables/candidate-card-iterations.html, iterations 1 and 6 Data: 6 candidates from the Candidates page
A

The track as the header

Decision first, with its ticks replaced by the story's dots, and one line under them: the stage, its days, and what comes next. Then who, AiMY's sentence and the verb.

From 1

  • Stage first, verb full-width at the foot, ⋯ for the rest.
  • Equal heights.

From 6

  • The dots and "→ next stage".

Watch

  • The stage is said twice: in the tag and the step line, so the tag goes.
B

The track as the card's top edge

The stages become the card's own top border, filled as far as they have got, the current segment in its tone. The head line names the stage and its days with "4 of 12"; the rest of the card is decision first.

From 1

  • The whole layout.

From 6

  • The track, as an edge: zero extra height.

Watch

  • A 5px edge is easy to miss; it reads as decoration until learned.
C

The verb says where it leads

The story's "what comes next" moves into the button itself: Agreed to talk with moves them to Applied under it. The track sits at the top; the last touch sits over the foot.

From 1

  • The verb owns the foot.

From 6

  • Next stage and last touch.

Watch

  • A two-line button is taller; long verbs wrap.
  • "Call" doesn't move anyone by itself; it says what the call can lead to.
D · Recommended

Two zones: who they are, then where they are

The card splits in two. The top is the person: name, role, one line of facts, AiMY's sentence. The bottom is a tinted status panel: the track, the stage with its days and what's next, the sentence of where they stand, and the verb. Amber when they've been in the stage longer than usual.

From 1

  • The decision zone is fixed at the foot of every card, at one height, so a row lines up.
  • Verb full-width, ⋯ for Not progressing.

From 6

  • The track, "→ next", and where they stand, all in the status zone.

Why this one

  • Two questions, two places: "should we?" above, "what now?" below.
  • The tint carries the late state without another badge.
  • The zone is the same component the profile's track uses.
E

Where they stand leads

The story's sentence ("Interviewed yesterday. The interviewer has not sent the result.") becomes the headline under the name, with a chip for who it waits on. The track and AiMY's sentence follow; the verb closes.

From 1

  • Verb at the foot, equal heights.

From 6

  • The status sentence, promoted.
  • The track.

Watch

  • Two bold lines (name and status) compete.
  • The "waits on" chip repeats the page's cut chips.
F

Compact: a labelled track, facts as chips

The shortest card. The track names only where they are and where they go next, under its ends. Facts are chips, AiMY's sentence is held to two lines and opens in full on hover.

From 1

  • Verb and ⋯ at the foot.

From 6

  • The track, labelled now → next.

Watch

  • Two lines of AiMY can cut "I'd check…", the useful half.
G

Side by side: the person left, the story right

A wider card in two columns. Left: who, AiMY's sentence and the verb. Right: a small vertical story of last, now and next, the same three moments the story card had, stacked.

From 1

  • Verb under the person, ⋯ beside it.

From 6

  • Last, now and next as a little timeline.

Watch

  • Needs ~400px a card: two or three to a row, not four.
  • Loses the whole-path view; only three moments.

The recommendation

Mix D. It keeps decision first's promise (the verb in the same place on every card, every card the same height) and gives the story card a home of its own instead of squeezing it into the header. From C, borrow the verb's "→ next" only where a press moves the stage (Agreed to talk, Passed); a Call button keeps its one word. On the candidate page, the same status panel can sit above the full track, so card and page match.
