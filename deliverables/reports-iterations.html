AiMY Talent · Reports · design iterations

From a grid of figures to a story

Today the Reports page is thirteen equal cards, then a funnel, then lists. Every number is right and defined, and none of them says what is happening. Below are six ways to tell it as a story, each drawn with the page’s real figures, with what inspired it and where it falls short. Number 6 is the recommendation.

4 Nov 2026 Data: Omar Saeed’s requisitions, opened in the last 90 days (10 requisitions, 12 seats) References: Mobbin + web research
Before drawing: what is the story?

The four things the figures say, once they are read together

1 · No step is slow. Six steps in a row are.

The first profile takes 19 days against a target of 7. But every step before it runs near its usual time: sourced 3.4 days (usual 3), applied 2.2 (2), assessment 3 (3), phone 2.2 (2), HR 3 (3), technical 4.3 (4). Added up that is 18 days. The delay is the length of the path, not one bottleneck. The card says “12 days over”; the story says why.

2 · The funnel beats its norms until the offer.

More people move on than usual at every step (HR 71% vs 54%, technical 86% vs 79%, client interviews 39% of profiles vs a 25% norm). Then the offer: 3 of 7 declined, 57% moving on against a usual 86%.

3 · The longest wait sits with the client.

8 of 15 days past the usual time wait on someone else’s answer, 6 of them on two profiles with the client. The other 7 are steps that are yours.

4 · After the hire, most stay, not enough.

18 of 23 earlier hires stayed to day 90: 78% against 85%. And clients who passed named salary most (4 of 12).

Why this matters for the design

A story needs a plot before it needs a chart. These four lines are the plot; each iteration below is a different way to tell the same four lines.

What must not be lost

Every one of today’s 13 figures, its definition (“How it is counted”), its target and its 3-span trend. The story leads; the figures stay one press away.
V1

The journey: everyone, from sourced to accepted

One diagram for the whole period: a band per stage that narrows as people leave, with AiMY’s notes pinned to the two places the story turns.

125 people found, 4 accepted. The funnel keeps more people than usual at every step, then loses 3 of 7 at the offer, the one step behind its norm.every requisition in view · moved on is of the people decided at each step

Inspired by

Pin · Pipeline Overview (Mobbin) Churnkey · Session outcome flow (Mobbin) Mailchimp · funnel with drop-off arrows (Mobbin)

Works

  • The whole period in one glance; the narrowing is the plot.
  • Notes sit on the diagram, where the eye already is.
  • Replaces the funnel table and two cards.

Falls short

  • Says nothing about time, which is the biggest problem (19 days).
  • The tail is thin: 9, 7 and 4 are hard to read at true scale.
V2

The 19 days: where the time to the first profile goes

One number, unfolded. The “Days to send profiles” card becomes a ruler: each step laid end to end at its real length, the target marked where it runs out.

Every step runs within half a day of its usual time; there are just six of them before a profile can go. The 7-day target runs out during the assessment. Sending a profile after HR, while the technical interview is booked, would bring it to about 14 days, and about 11 where there is no assessment.days in each stage, the requisitions in view · a suggestion to test, not a measured result

Inspired by

Arcade · steps as bars with a headline sentence (Mobbin) Wrike · Gantt strip (Mobbin) and the waterfall chart (finance’s “bridge”): target to actual, step by step.

Works

  • Turns the worst figure into a reason, and the reason into a move.
  • Honest: it shows the steps are fine, so nobody is blamed for a slow step that is not slow.

Falls short

  • One figure only. A page of these is long.
  • Averages laid end to end are a model, not one person’s real path; the footnote has to say so.
V3

Who it waits on: the late days, by desk

“Where time goes” as swimlanes. One lane per person the work waits on, the steps across, a circle wherever days have piled up past the usual time.

8 of 15 late days wait on someone else’s answer, and 6 of those are two profiles with Northwind’s hiring team. Your own 7 are spread thin: no single step of yours is more than 3 days behind.the open requisitions in view, nowNudge Northwind on the 2 profiles

Inspired by

Trello · lanes by status over time (Mobbin) Productboard · swimlane timeline (Mobbin) and service blueprints: one lane per actor.

Works

  • Answers “whose desk is it on?” at a glance, the question the page exists for.
  • Ends in a move: the nudge.
  • Counts your own steps too, so it is not a chart of other people to blame.

Falls short

  • About now, not the period: it reads differently from the rest of the page.
  • Empty lanes (hiring manager, candidate) take room.

Why this one

  • The story is said once, at the top, and every block below proves one line of it.
  • Three different diagrams for three different questions: how many (journey), how long (ruler), whose desk (lanes). No two blocks repeat a figure.
  • Nothing from today’s page is lost: the 13 figures and their definitions keep their place, one press down.
  • Each chapter ends in a move, so the report is also a to-do.

Rules it keeps

  • The headline is built from the figures and cites them; no figure appears in words that is not in a diagram below.
  • Colour carries meaning only: amber off target, green on, violet the flow. Every coloured figure is also said in words.
  • Changes with the period and account filters, as the page does today.
  • A desk sees its own story: the HR Manager’s headline is about the team, the hiring manager’s about their requests.
