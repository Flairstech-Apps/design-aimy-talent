What gets in the way of hiring, and how AiMY Talent fixes it

The everyday problems recruiters, hiring managers, HR and candidates run into, each with what Talent does about it.

A recruiter's day

For recruiters

  • The problem

    With more applications and fewer people, it's hard to know where to start each morning.

    What Talent does

    A daily briefing opens with the decisions waiting on you and how long the oldest has waited, then only what's stuck or clashing, each with a suggested next step you approve in one tap.

    Shown in the prototype
  • The problem

    Recruiters drag every candidate across a board, so nothing moves unless they move it.

    What Talent does

    There's no board. AiMY keeps every application up to date from what actually happens: a booked interview or a piece of feedback moves it on by itself.

    Planned
  • The problem

    It's hard to tell what's already been done for you, and what's due today.

    What Talent does

    The briefing lists what AiMY did for you since yesterday, and today's interviews and promises.

    Shown in the prototype
  • The problem

    Understanding one candidate means piecing together emails, notes and calls.

    What Talent does

    Open any candidate to see one timeline: what happened, the evidence behind it, what was promised, and what should happen next.

    Shown in the prototype

Finding the right people

For recruiters

  • The problem

    Good candidates are already in our database, but search can't find them, and the same person can show up twice.

    What Talent does

    One clean record per person, searchable in plain words. Strong matches from people we already know come up for each open role, with what happened last time.

    Shown in the prototype
  • The problem

    CV uploads fail, and applications arrive in different places, so some applicants never get in.

    What Talent does

    Every application lands in one place, whether it came from the website, the chat on our site, email or an upload.

    Planned
  • The problem

    A match score says “72%” but not why, so good people get skipped over a number.

    What Talent does

    No percentages. Talent shows what the CV proves for each must-have, and what simply isn't known yet.

    Shown in the prototype

Testing language and skills

For recruiters, hiring managers and candidates

  • The problem

    Candidates who already have an IELTS or TOEFL result are tested all over again.

    What Talent does

    It reads the certificate and only tests what's still missing.

    Planned
  • The problem

    Tests can feel unfair: pitched at the wrong level, or marked down for an accent.

    What Talent does

    The test adjusts to each person's level, and speaking is judged on being understood, never on accent.

    Planned
  • The problem

    A borderline score or a copied answer could decide someone's chances automatically.

    What Talent does

    Nobody is rejected by the system. Unclear or flagged results go to a person, who sees who's waiting at a glance.

    Shown in the prototype
  • The problem

    Seeing “Fail” puts people off, even when they'd suit another role.

    What Talent does

    Candidates see “Not yet”, and Talent suggests other roles they qualify for.

    Planned
  • The problem

    One badly written question quietly drags a whole test down.

    What Talent does

    It spots the questions nearly everyone gets wrong and suggests a better wording for HR to approve.

    Shown in the prototype

Keeping candidates in the loop

For recruiters and candidates

  • The problem

    Candidates wait days for news, or never hear back after an interview.

    What Talent does

    AiMY sends the routine updates and reminds the recruiter when someone is owed an answer. A person always sends the “no”.

    Shown in the prototype
  • The problem

    Approving every single message slows everything down, but not everyone wants WhatsApp.

    What Talent does

    Approve a kind of message once and AiMY handles the rest, asking only before a first contact or a reply it wrote itself. WhatsApp only with permission, email otherwise.

    Shown in the prototype

Interviews and decisions

For recruiters and hiring managers

  • The problem

    Booking interviews and chasing feedback eats days of back and forth.

    What Talent does

    AiMY books from the calendar and chases feedback. Feedback is a two-minute chat, and each interviewer answers before seeing the others.

    Shown in the prototype
  • The problem

    Hiring managers get flooded with candidates who haven't been screened.

    What Talent does

    They see only the people the recruiter has put forward, and the few things that need their answer.

    Shown in the prototype

Offers and the first 90 days

For recruiters, hiring managers and new hires

  • The problem

    An offer waits on one approver, then the next, and the salary gap shows up late.

    What Talent does

    Both approvers sign off the same offer at the same time, with the salary asked for shown next to the budget. A person sends it.

    Shown in the prototype
  • The problem

    Getting a new hire set up, from laptop to access, is done by hand.

    What Talent does

    An accepted offer starts an onboarding plan, including the request to IT.

    Planned
  • The problem

    Early feedback about a new hire could be used against them.

    What Talent does

    The 30- and 90-day check-ins only help us hire better overall. They're never used in anyone's probation.

    Planned

Running the team

For the HR manager and recruiters

  • The problem

    A role is going to miss its date and nobody knows until it does, and pushing the deadline hides it.

    What Talent does

    Roles are ranked by their risk of missing the date, with plain reasons such as too few people on the shortlist or slow interviewers. Next: showing how often each date was moved.

    Shown in the prototype
  • The problem

    An AI that changes its own rules can drift without anyone noticing.

    What Talent does

    AiMY only suggests changes. Nothing changes until the HR manager approves it.

    Shown in the prototype

Still being worked out
