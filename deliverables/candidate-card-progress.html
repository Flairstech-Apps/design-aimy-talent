Why AiMY's insight leads

What changes

  • Second on the card, under the name, not after the facts.
  • A verdict chip: Strong, Partly fits, Not shown yet.
  • The must-haves as chips under the sentence: filled shown, dashed partly, grey not known yet.
  • The words AiMY found are tinted in its sentence.

Honest by design

  • No score, as decided on 29 Oct: the verdict is words and the chips are the evidence.
  • "Not known yet" is grey, not red: the CV is silent, which is not a no.
1

Ticks, as today, with the stage and next named

Today's segmented ticks, with a line above them naming the stage, its days and the next stage.

Works

  • Familiar; the smallest change.

Falls short

  • 12 ticks are tiny; the count does the work.

Inspired by

Walmart · order stepssegments with the current one named
2

One fill bar

A single bar filled as far as they've got, the end in the stage's tone, amber when they've been at it longer than usual. The words above it say which stage and what next.

Works

  • Calmest: one shape, readable at a glance.

Falls short

  • Hides how many steps there are.

Inspired by

Apple · order at a glancea thin bar under the sentenceCoinbase · applicationa fill bar over the steps
3

Dots, like the profile's track

The candidate page's track, small: one dot per stage, passed filled, the current one ringed. The card and the page draw the same thing.

Works

  • Same picture as the profile, so the two agree.
  • Shows the whole path and where they are on it.

Falls short

  • 12 dots crowd a narrow card.

Inspired by

Zillow · lease stepsdots on a line, the last ringed
4 · Recommended

Phases: screening, then the client, then the offer

The stages grouped into the three parts of the job, each a short segmented bar with its name under it: Screening (yours), Client or Manager (theirs), Offer. The current phase's name lights up; the current stage is named above with its days.

Works

  • Says whose part it's in, not just how far.
  • Three names read where twelve ticks can't.
  • FlairsTech's own roles show Manager instead of Client.

Falls short

  • A new idea: phases aren't named anywhere else yet.

Inspired by

Salesforce · pathnamed stages along one barSelfridges · resale stepsnumbered phases with names
5

Time in the stage

The bar measures days, not stages: it fills with the days at the current stage against its usual time (the dashed mark), turning amber past it. A hairline of ticks under it still shows the whole path.

Works

  • A stuck candidate shows itself.

Falls short

  • Two bars to read; the path is secondary.

Inspired by

Pipedrive · days per stagetime is the stage's label
6

Before, now, next

Only three steps: the one they passed, the one they're at (ringed, with days), and the next. The rest of the path is implied.

Works

  • Every step is named; nothing to count.
  • Answers "what happens next" on the card.

Falls short

  • No sense of how far along overall.

Inspired by

Mercor · processing stepsthree named steps, the middle one live
7

A ring beside the stage

A small ring that fills as they go, "2/12" inside it, next to the stage, its days and what comes next.

Works

  • Compact; the number is the read.

Falls short

  • A ring reads as a score; scores were ruled out on 29 Oct.

Inspired by

Workable · match ringa small ring beside the heading

The recommendation

AiMY's insight under the name, as drawn, in every case. For the progress bar, variation 4 (phases): it is the only one that says whose part the candidate is in, which is what decides who acts next, and three named segments read faster than twelve ticks. Variation 3 (dots) is the safe alternative, as it matches the profile's track.
