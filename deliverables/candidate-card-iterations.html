Seven ways to draw a candidate card

Today's card stacks eight things in one column: the stage and source, the name and role, two lines of facts, an earlier application, AiMY's paragraph, the ticks, where they stand, and two buttons. Everything is there, and the one thing to do is at the bottom of a tall card. Each iteration below is drawn with the same six real candidates from Omar Saeed's queue (4 Nov), with what inspired it and where it falls short. Iteration 1 is the recommendation.

4 Nov 2026 Data: 6 candidates from the Candidates page, as the app draws them References: Mobbin (20 screens)
Today

Everything, in one column

What is right

  • AiMY's sentence says why they fit and what to check, in its own voice.
  • One verb per stage at the foot: Agreed to talk, Call, Passed.
  • The ticks show how far along they are.

What is not

  • The action sits at the bottom of a ~330px card.
  • Facts take two lines that say little to compare by.
  • Cards of unequal height in a row.

Must stay

  • AiMY's sentence, the stage, how long, the one verb, Not progressing.
  • Returning candidates flagged.
1 · Recommended

Decision first: the stage and the verb frame the card

The stage, its ticks and how long they've been there open the card; the name and one line of facts follow; AiMY's sentence stays, in its own voice, without the box around it; the stage's one verb fills the foot, with Not progressing and the rest behind ⋯. Every card is the same height.

Inspired by

Workable · candidate overview"Next up" leads, the one move on top Braintrust · matched talentone row of facts, the action at the right Care.com · applicantsone primary verb, the "no" as a quiet link

Works

  • Scan a row by stage and days, act without reading.
  • ~40% shorter; equal heights.
  • Keeps every part that must stay.

Falls short

  • Not progressing is one press further away.
  • Location and languages merge into one grey line.
2

The evidence: one chip per must-have

The must-haves become chips: filled where the CV shows it, dashed where it partly does, grey where it doesn't say. AiMY keeps one sentence, the part about what to check. You compare people by the shape of their chips.

Inspired by

Pin · candidate criteriaeach criterion checked against the profile Wellfound · applicants"✓ 10 years of exp" matched requirement chip Workable · how they match✓ met, ✕ not shown, per criterion Braintrust · skill chipsmatched skills as tags on the card

Works

  • Fit at a glance, and honest: "not known yet" is not "missing".
  • Talent drew these chips before (whyTags), so the parts exist.

Falls short

  • Long must-haves wrap into tall chip clouds.
  • Less of AiMY's voice, which was asked for on 4 Nov.
3

The list: one row per person, AiMY's line highlighted

A dense row: who, the stage with its ticks, AiMY's sentence with what they show highlighted and what to check underlined, the verb at the right. Twice as many people per screen; the cards stay as an option.

Inspired by

Juicebox · PeopleGPT resultsAI summary with the matched words highlighted Juicebox · profiles listone row each, the shortlist button at the right Apollo · find peopledense rows with one action column

Works

  • Fastest to scan a long queue; highlights make the sentence skimmable.

Falls short

  • A table look; the Candidates page was set to cards only (29 Oct).
  • Needs ~1000px; narrower screens fall back to cards.
4

The board: columns by who the next move waits on

The page's own cuts as columns: waiting on you, first contact, with the client, waiting on others. Cards shrink to name, role, stage and days, and the verb. The column says what kind of work it is.

Inspired by

Wrangle · pipeline boardcompact cards with quick icons, per stage Juicebox · board by statusNot contacted → Interested columns folk · recruitment pipelineinitial avatars, three facts per card Homerun · candidates board"Review" status on each card

Works

  • The cut chips become the layout; no filter needed.
  • Your column is your to-do.

Falls short

  • AiMY's sentence doesn't fit; it moves to hover or the profile.
  • Four columns need width; a phone shows one at a time.
5

Hover to read: a short face, the reasoning on hover or pin

At rest the card is short: who, stage and days, the verb. AiMY's sentence, the facts and the earlier application open on hover, and a press on the pin keeps them open. Hover a card to try it.

Inspired by

Wrangle · compact cardsa short face, detail in the side panel Peerlist · people gridtwo lines and one button per card

Works

  • Twice the cards per screen; detail is one hover away.
  • Matches the hover-or-pin rule used elsewhere in Talent.

Falls short

  • No hover on touch: a tap has to open it.
  • AiMY's voice is hidden by default.
6

The story card: where they are, what's next, what happened last

The candidate page's track, small: dots for the requisition's stages with the current one named and the next one beside it. Under it, the last touch and what is owed. Reads as a status, like an order card.

Inspired by

Workable · "Next up"the next scheduled step on the card Lightfield · opportunity cardslast interaction and next step as lines Notion · board cardslabelled property lines

Works

  • Same picture as the profile's track, so the two pages agree.
  • "Next: Phone screen" says what the verb leads to.

Falls short

  • No reasons for fit on the face.
  • 12 dots get tight on narrow cards.
7

Quick contact: the person first, the ways to reach them on the card

For the screening stages, where the job is calling: initials, name and role, the number, and call / WhatsApp / email as icons on the card. Location and languages as chips; AiMY's sentence under them.

Inspired by

Wrangle · cards with contact iconsLinkedIn, email, add, delete on each card Juicebox · contact icons by the namephone, email, LinkedIn beside the name Slack · peoplethe person first, Message and Huddle under

Works

  • The call is one press from the list.
  • Shows when WhatsApp is off (no opt-in) before you try.

Falls short

  • Only right for the early stages; later stages want 1.
  • A phone number on every card is a lot of personal data on screen.

The recommendation

Iteration 1 as the card: it keeps every part that has to stay and moves the decision to where the eye starts. Two borrowings: iteration 2's chips as a hover on AiMY's sentence, so the evidence is one hover away without crowding the face; and iteration 7's call icon on the foot for Applied and Phone screen, where calling is the verb.
