Seven shorter candidate cards

Today's card is about 330px tall and the two-zone card was still about 250px; both read as too long. Every direction below aims at about half today's height or less, measured on its heading. Each keeps what has to stay (the name, the stage and how long, AiMY's sentence, the verb, Not progressing) and moves the rest behind a hover or a press. Same six candidates from Omar Saeed's queue. The measured height is on each heading.

4 Nov 2026 Replaces mix D, which was reverted References: Mobbin (16 screens)
1

Two lines

Line one: the name and role, the verb at the right. Line two: the stage and days, then AiMY's sentence clipped to one line; hover the card for all of it. Not progressing is a quiet link under the verb.

Inspired by

Maze · review applicantsname, status and the action on one line Wrangle · shared shortlisttwo lines a person, View at the right

Works

  • The shortest card that still says why.
  • The verb is where the eye starts, top right.

Falls short

  • One line of AiMY usually cuts "I'd check…".
  • Facts (years, place, languages) only on the profile.
2 · Recommended

The strip: one wide row a person

One full-width card a row, laid out sideways: who on the left, AiMY's sentence in the middle (two lines, all of it on hover), the stage and the verb on the right. Width replaces height.

Inspired by

Care.com · applicantsperson, note, then Message and "no thanks" at the right Workable · candidateswho on the left, job status on the right Upwork · talentwide rows, Invite at the right

Works

  • AiMY gets two full lines and still the card is short.
  • Reads left to right: who, why, what now.

Falls short

  • One to a row: it reads like a list of cards.
  • On a phone it stacks back into a taller card.
3

AiMY's sentence is the card

No fact lines. The name and role, then AiMY's sentence as the body, in full. A single foot line: the stage and days on the left, the verb on the right, Not progressing as a link. Years, place and languages open on hovering the name.

Inspired by

Juicebox · profile shortlistthe AI paragraph is the body of each card Polywork · people cardsname, a short paragraph, a line of tags Base44 · top matchesparagraph, then one Contact at the foot

Works

  • Keeps AiMY's whole sentence in its own voice, which you asked for.
  • Every part that must stay is still on the face.
  • Still ~190–215px: shorter, but not by half.
  • One foot line, so a row lines up on its verbs.

Falls short

  • The facts need a hover.
  • Cards with long sentences are a line taller.
4

Tiles: a face, a name, a verb

A small tile, five or six to a row: initials, name, role, stage and days, the verb. Everything else opens in a card over the tile on hover.

Inspired by

ClickUp · team tilesinitials tile, name, status dot Slack · peopleface tiles, detail in a panel Whop · user cardsone Follow under each person

Works

  • Most people per screen of any card.
  • Good for First contact: approve a batch by face and name.

Falls short

  • Says nothing about fit until hovered.
  • Not progressing only in the hover card.
5

The accordion: a row that opens in place

Each person is one slim bar: name and role, stage and days, the verb, and a chevron. Pressing the bar opens AiMY's sentence and the facts under it, inside the list. Press a row to try it.

Inspired by

Xero · duplicate billsa summary row that expands into its details Babbel · lessonsslim rows, a chevron each

Works

  • About 56px a person closed.
  • Detail opens where you are; no hover, so it works on touch.

Falls short

  • A list, not cards; the page was set to cards on 29 Oct.
  • Open rows push the rest down.
6

A chip line and a verdict

The facts collapse into one row of chips with the stage first. AiMY speaks in a verdict of a few words, "Partly fits · check NetSuite and English", with the full sentence on hover. The verb and Not progressing share the foot.

Inspired by

Wellfound · candidatesone line of facts under the name Polywork · tags rowfacts as a row of small tags Uxcel · screeninga short verdict beside each person

Works

  • Every fact stays on the face, in one row.
  • The verdict reads in a second.

Falls short

  • A verdict of a few words is less AiMY's voice.
  • Chips wrap to two rows on narrow cards.
7

Grouped by requisition, one line each

The requisition the cards repeat becomes a heading, and under it each person is one line: who, stage and days, AiMY's sentence in a line, the verb. Shorter because the shared parts are said once.

Inspired by

Wrangle · shortlist per rolea role heading, then its people Workable · candidatesjob status beside each person

Works

  • About 52px a person; the role is said once.
  • Matches how a recruiter thinks: by requisition.

Falls short

  • A list, not cards.
  • The cut chips (waiting on you, with the client) cross the groups.

The recommendation

Direction 2, the strip. At about 80px a person it is a quarter of today's card, and it still shows everything that has to stay: who, two lines of AiMY's sentence, the stage and its days, the verb and Not progressing. One card to a row instead of four, yet more people per screen, because each is a quarter of the height. If the page must keep four to a row, direction 1 (two lines, ~127px) is next; direction 3 keeps AiMY's whole sentence but only gets to about two-thirds of today's height.
