AiMY Talent UI/UX plan

26 October 2026 · Engy · Sources: the knowledge vault, the prototype on main, and the market research

Fix the prototype in three waves: first the changes that make the hiring loop learn (client decline reasons, honest insights, safe sourcing, WhatsApp opt-in), then the open audit gaps on each desk, then the outcome-linked work that needs months of data.

Design rules

Every screen keeps the AiMY Sales rules the prototype already follows, plus four guardrails the research added.

Kept from AiMY Sales and the product overview

  • One press with Undo, never “are you sure”. Prose sits behind Edit; no form is the only way.
  • The stage is stored and moves forward only; an exit is a reason, not a stage.
  • One definition per figure, each with its unit, start event and stop event.
  • Evidence as counts (“4 of 5 must-haves met”), never a bare match percentage.
  • Nothing is public by default; a requisition reaches candidates only when flagged Published.
  • The hiring manager sees only what the recruiter advanced; the client sees nothing internal.

Added by the research

  • Design evidence to be checked, not trusted. People follow AI picks about 90% of the time even when they are wrong, so unknowns are shown as unknown and a sample of the knockout lane is reviewed.
  • AI fills facts, people give the verdict. AiMY never pre-fills a rating or a proceed decision.
  • No ranking of individual recruiters in Talent; team and request views only.
  • Consent before contact and before recording. A web-found person is a prospect until they reply; WhatsApp only after opt-in; no emotion read from face or voice.

Component patterns

Four patterns carry this plan; each mockup is a spec drawn in the AiMY Sales style with invented people. The other seven (sourcing, stage templates, the client view, assessment items, insights, day 90, consent) are in the vault’s Research results canvas.

Client decision with a reason (P1)

  • The sendout is its own record: sent, opened, decided, each dated.
  • Pass asks for exactly one reason from a fixed list; “Other” takes one line of text.
  • AiMY says what the reason now feeds (“4th decline this month for spoken German”) and that it feeds matching for this client only.

Evidence per criterion

  • The roll-up is a count (“4 of 5 required met”), never a percentage.
  • Each criterion shows Meets, Not met or Not known, with the CV passage quoted; “The CV is silent here” is not Not met.
  • An unknown offers one action: ask the candidate. The footer names the criteria version and who changed it.

Debrief: AI fills facts, you give the verdict

  • Transcript quotes are labelled “check it” and are editable; the verdict starts empty and AiMY never fills it.
  • A gap warning names the template topic that did not come up and offers to ask by message.
  • Others’ feedback stays hidden until this interviewer submits.

Verified skills over time

  • Columns: skill, CV says, interview found, status (Confirmed, Differs, Not checked), evidence with a link to the moment.
  • AiMY suggests each finding; the interviewer confirms it before it lands.
  • The candidate sees and edits only their own layer: what the CV says.

Validation

Run the client-feedback study first: it decides the reason codes in P1 and what matching must learn.

  • Code the client feedback on the last 50 profiles sent: skills, language, salary, notice or other. Decides the P1 reason list.
  • Shadow 5 recruiters for a day and count actions by type. Decides how much of Today is briefing and how much is records.
  • Run an assessment before the call on one high-volume support role for 2 weeks; compare recruiter hours and drop-off. Decides whether assessments are Now or Later.
  • Think-aloud with 5 hiring managers, starting with the one who holds 20 requests. Decides M11 and where asks reach them.
  • Ask 2 account managers how clients send feedback today. Decides the client screen’s channel.
  • Walk every Wave 1 flow in the prototype in the browser at 1536 px and at phone width before writing tickets.
